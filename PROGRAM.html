Low volume, high intensity, high frequency. Two hard sets per exercise, every muscle twice a week, sessions of half an hour. Built for an adjustable bench, two 40 kg dumbbells, a pull-up bar, and a backpack.

The rotation

Run the six sessions in order. Take a rest day whenever you want one and continue from where you left off. Default is six on, one off. If you feel fresh, keep going. If your set 1 numbers drop two sessions in a row, rest.

How every set is done

Two working sets

Set 1 stops with 1 rep in reserve. Set 2 goes to concentric failure: you cannot complete another rep with good form. Stop before form breaks down, not after.

Warm up once

Only the first compound of the session gets warm-up sets: about 50% for 8 reps, then 75% for 4. Everything after that is already warm.

Slow, controlled reps

One second up, two to three seconds down, no bounce. This is what makes two sets enough, and what makes 40 kg dumbbells enough.

Rest fully

Two to three minutes between compound sets, about 90 seconds on isolation work. If a session runs past 35 minutes, you are resting too long or adding exercises.

The six sessions

Reps are a range. When set 1 hits the top of the range, add weight next time. Weights are per dumbbell.

Progression

Double progression. Each exercise has a rep range. When set 1 reaches the top of the range with good form, add weight next session and start again from the bottom of the range. Dumbbell steps are usually 2 to 2.5 kg per hand. Only set 1 decides this. Set 2 is to failure and will vary.

Example. Flat press, 2 × 6–10. You get 32 kg × 10 and × 8. Set 1 hit 10, so next time use 34 kg. You get 34 × 7 and × 6. Keep 34 until set 1 reaches 10 again.

When the weights run out

The Mentzer answer to limited equipment: raise intensity, do not add sets. Work down this list in order. Adding sets is the last option.

  1. Slow the eccentric to 4–5 seconds

    A 40 kg Romanian deadlift with a five-second lowering is a different exercise.

  2. Pause at the hardest point

    Two seconds at the bottom of a press, squat, or Romanian deadlift.

  3. Go single-leg or single-arm

    Half the muscle, same dumbbell. Bulgarian split squat, single-leg Romanian deadlift, single-leg hip thrust, single-leg calf raise.

  4. Load the backpack

    Pull-ups, chin-ups, push-ups, and dips between the bench and a chair all take up to +40 kg.

  5. Rest-pause on set 2

    Hit failure, rest 15 seconds, do more reps, rest 15 seconds, do more. One set, three mini-failures.

  6. Then, and only then, a third or fourth set

    Mainly for the hinge movements, where load is most limited.

Deadlift. A conventional dumbbell deadlift at 2 × 40 kg will feel easy, so it is not in the plan. The Romanian deadlift and single-leg Romanian deadlift replace it. They load the hamstrings harder with less weight and keep the lower back honest.

Rest and recovery

Rest days are yours to place

The rotation does not care which day of the week it is. Default is one rest day per week.

Deload on a signal, or every 8 weeks

Deload when set 1 numbers drop two sessions in a row on two or more exercises, or every eight weeks, whichever comes first. A deload is four to five days off, or one full rotation at one set per exercise with two reps in reserve.

Sleep and food make two sets work

If you are under-recovered, the symptom is stalled numbers. Fix recovery before touching the program.

Joint pain is a stop sign

Swap the exercise and come back later. Flat press to push-up, overhead press to incline press, pull-up to row.

Logging

Write every working set down: date, session, exercise, weight, reps for set 1 and set 2. Progression is decided by the log, not by feel. Beating the last entry is the whole program.
